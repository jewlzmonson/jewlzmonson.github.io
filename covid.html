COVID-19 Global Analysis

An end-to-end analytics project combining SQL data engineering, an interactive Tableau dashboard, and original survey research — exploring how the COVID-19 vaccination rollout related to shifts in death-rate trends across countries and over time, while taking a careful look at where public-health data can and can’t be trusted.

The question

When COVID-19 vaccines became widely available, their impact was hotly debated — and often politicized. This project sets that debate against the data: two global datasets from Our World in Data (deaths and vaccinations) plus an original Qualtrics survey, to explore the relationship between vaccination rollout and mortality trends, and to surface the data-quality issues that make that relationship harder to read than it looks.

Three research questions guided the project:

1
Did the COVID-19 vaccination rollout correlate with reductions in mortality over time?
2
Which countries were hit hardest, and when did vaccines actually arrive there?
3
What does the global and continental picture look like across the pandemic timeline?

What the data showed

Framed as exploration of relationships in observational data — not a causal claim about the vaccine.

Death-rate trends shifted alongside rollout

Comparing a pre-vaccination era to an early-vaccination era across the five focus countries, the case-fatality rate trended downward in most after vaccines were introduced — consistent with, though not proof of, a protective effect. The relationship is clearly time-lagged: changes show up well after rollout begins.

Impact was deeply uneven by region

Europe, North America, and South America carried the heaviest reported death counts, while the infection-rate map shows the United States, United Kingdom, and parts of Europe with the highest share of population infected — useful for thinking about where limited vaccine supply would matter most.

Rollout timing varied widely

The rolling-vaccination view makes the staggered global timeline obvious: wealthier countries ramped first, with much of the world following months later — a key caveat when reading any country-to-country mortality comparison.

Reported data has real blind spots

Cross-country differences in how a “COVID-19 death” is defined and recorded mean the raw numbers understate true mortality in places with weaker registration systems — which is why the analysis leans on five countries with strong civil-registration data while keeping the global picture for context.

How it was built

Secondary data engineered in SQL; primary data collected and cleaned from an original survey.

Secondary data — SQL

  • Removed continent-level aggregate rows with WHERE continent IS NOT NULL to prevent double-counting deaths.
  • Cast text-typed measures to numeric (CAST(... AS BIGINT/FLOAT)) so they could be aggregated.
  • Guarded against early-pandemic divide-by-zero errors using NULLIF(total_cases, 0).
  • Built a rolling cumulative vaccination total with a window function: SUM(...) OVER (PARTITION BY location ORDER BY date).
  • Used CTEs and ranking/lag functions for pre- vs. post-vaccination era comparisons.
  • Packaged the results into six Tableau-ready views.

Primary data — survey

  • Original Qualtrics survey distributed to adults 18+ on vaccination attitudes and outcomes.
  • Captured doses received, booster intent, and trust drivers (health, government, media, medical advice) on a 7-point scale.
  • Included an open-ended question for qualitative detail on what shaped each decision.
  • Cleaned to 40 valid responses — removing previews, incompletes, an under-18 respondent, and blanks.
  • Used to compare local attitudes against documented behavioral research on vaccine uptake.
